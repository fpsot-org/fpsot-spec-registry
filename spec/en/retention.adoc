= Retention and destruction
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A filing is a legal record.
The law of its register and the retention schedule of its body govern it.
An audit event is an operational record, and the observability specification governs it.
A filing emits events, but it is not one, and the two need different retention.
The evidence that supports a filing often needs a third, shorter class.
The Québec enterprise registrar destroys the copy of the identity document of a director once the declaration is on file.

Destruction is not hypothetical either.
In 2012, the link:https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/AnnualStatutes/2012_6/[Ending the Long-gun Registry Act], S.C. 2012, c. 6, ordered the government to destroy the long-gun records.
The link:https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/F-11.6/rpdc.html[Firearms Act] now ties that destruction to a copy for Québec, _to be verified_.

A blanket rule of no deletion would be unlawful there.
A silent deletion would break the chain and the audit trail.
The answer rests on the salted hashes of the signed records specification.

== Retention classes

[[REG-090]]
*REG-090.* Each filing type and each evidence type of a register MUST have a retention class in its profile.
Each class MUST name the rule of the retention schedule that it applies, as OBS-102 does for log stores.
A {term-legal-hold} under OBS-103 MUST stop the register from destroying each record that it covers.

The `ca-federal` profile names the disposition authorities of Library and Archives Canada, _to be verified_.
The `ca-qc` profile names the retention schedule of the registrar.
Its class for identity copies ends once a filing completes.

[[REG-091]]
*REG-091.* A register MUST NOT keep a filing only as long as the events that it emits.
The periods that OBS-100 to OBS-104 set for audit events MUST NOT shorten the life of a filing.

The `ca-qc-floor` profile of OBS-101 sets defaults for log stores, not for legal records.
A register that applied it to filings would lose them years too early.

[[REG-092]]
*REG-092.* A filing MUST hold only references to its evidence.
Each reference MUST give the salted SHA-256 hash of the document and its retention class.
The register MUST keep the salt with the document, and destroy them together.
A register MAY destroy a document of evidence at the end of its class without a destruction filing.
The destruction MUST leave an event under OBS-104.

The filing stays whole when its evidence goes, and the hash still proves which document it used.
Without the salt, nobody can test a guess about a short document against the hash.
A whole filing needs a destruction filing, under REG-093, because its content shapes the register.

== Destruction filings

[[REG-093]]
*REG-093.* A register MUST destroy a filing only through a filing of the category `destruction`.
The destruction filing MUST give its legal basis, the authority that ordered it and the values that it targets.
It MUST name each target by its field and an opaque id, never by a personal value.
It MUST name every filing that holds a targeted value, corrections and later filings included.
It MUST destroy each of them, or list it as retained with the reason that the register cannot destroy it.
It MUST also give the instant at which the register checked for legal holds, and each copy made before.

The register MUST destroy the content and the salt of each named filing together, in every copy, under LOG-081.
It MUST emit an event under OBS-104 for each destruction.

Without the salt, nobody can test a guess against the commitment.
The commitment that stays in the chain and in the log then says nothing about the content.
A correction often repeats the values of the filing that it corrects.
Destroying the original alone would leave them in place.
A sealed filing cannot lose one field, so the register destroys every filing that holds a target.

[[REG-094]]
*REG-094.* Each destroyed filing MUST leave a tombstone in the class `Tombstone` of `model/registry.yaml`.
The tombstone MUST keep the sequence, the type, the category, the effective and completion instants and the commitment.
It MUST also keep the previous commitment and the ids of the rows that the filing opened and ended.
For a correction, it MUST keep the corrected filing and the ids of the later versions that it restated.
It MUST NOT keep any other value of the filing.

A tombstone keeps what the chain and the replay need, and nothing that could identify a person.

[[REG-095]]
*REG-095.* After a destruction, the light chain MUST still verify, under REG-032.
The replay MUST still give each snapshot whose filings remain whole.
A row that only destroyed content supports MUST appear as a destroyed row.
That row gives its kind, its id and its filing.
A register MUST NOT guess the values of such a row.

In the fixture, a lawful order targets an identity document that the issue and its first correction both held.
The destruction filing destroys both, and a second correction, without the document, restates the issue.
The chain verifies, because the second and fourth filings point to the commitments that the tombstones keep.
As of 20 June 2026, recorded on 30 June, the craft shows as a destroyed row.

[[REG-096]]
*REG-096.* A copy that a register makes before a destruction MUST appear in the destruction filing.
The filing MUST give its recipient category, its instant and its legal basis.
The copy is a disclosure, so REG-074 and REG-076 apply to it.

The Firearms Act ties the end of the long-gun records to a copy for Québec, _to be verified_.
A record of that copy shows what left the register before the rest disappeared.
