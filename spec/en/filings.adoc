= Filings
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A filing is one legal event in a register.
It can be an application that a party submits, or an act of the registrar.
It can also be a correction, an order of a court or a lawful destruction.

The registers that the research studied already work this way.
link:https://github.com/bcgov/lear[LEAR], the business register of British Columbia, stores each filing whole, with its filing, effective and completion dates.
It records a correction as a new filing that points at the original.
The link:https://github.com/bcgov/ppr[personal property register] of the same province makes each legal act a registration with a change class.
Amendment, court order, renewal and discharge are such classes.

This page sets the filing that every register keeps.
It also sets the light chain, which makes a gap or a reordering visible.

== One filing for each change

[[REG-020]]
*REG-020.* Each change to the state of a register MUST come from exactly one completed filing.
That holds for the actions of staff, for migrations and for corrections.
It holds for orders and for destructions too.
A system MUST NOT write register state in any other way.

A direct write leaves no legal event behind it.
Nobody can then say who changed what, when and under which power.

[[REG-021]]
*REG-021.* Each filing MUST be a record of the class `Filing` of `model/registry.yaml`.
Its filing type MUST come from the catalog of the domain pack of its register.
Each filing type MUST belong to one of three categories, which tell the replay how to apply it.

* `change` opens and ends rows of derived state.
* `correction` replaces an earlier filing, under REG-027.
* `destruction` destroys the content of earlier filings, under REG-093, and changes no row.

The catalog of the pleasure craft pack has eight types.
Issue, transfer, renewal, change of particulars and cancellation are of the `change` category.
So is migration, while correction and destruction have their own categories.

== Immutability

[[REG-022]]
*REG-022.* A register MUST NOT change the content of a filing once it receives it.
Until the filing reaches a final processing status, the register MAY change only its three statuses, `completedAt`, `sequence` and `previousCommitment`.
After a final status, the register MUST NOT change anything in the filing.
A later change in the facts MUST be a new filing.

The Québec enterprise register treats a correction as a filing type of its own, the corrective declaration.
A filing that can change in place would let the register rewrite the evidence of what a party declared.

== Three dates

[[REG-023]]
*REG-023.* Each filing MUST carry three instants under DAT-010.

* `submittedAt`: when the register received the filing.
* `effectiveAt`: when the legal act takes effect in the world, such as the day of a sale.
* `completedAt`: when the filing reached a final processing status.

A filing whose `effectiveAt` comes after its `completedAt` MUST change nothing before its `effectiveAt`.
Only a filing type that the catalog marks as retroactive may have an `effectiveAt` before its `submittedAt`.
A transfer and a correction are such types.

A buyer often files days after a sale, so the sale and the filing have different dates.
LEAR holds a future-effective filing until its effective date.
Both dates matter in law, and the completion date tells when the register knew.

== Three statuses

[[REG-024]]
*REG-024.* Each filing MUST carry three separate statuses: payment, review and processing.
The values of the payment and review statuses MUST come from the lists of the domain pack.
The processing status MUST be one of `received`, `in-progress`, `completed`, `rejected` or `withdrawn`, and the last three are final.
Only `completed` changes the state of the register.
A payment or a review status MUST NOT stand in for a processing status.

A paid filing can still fail review, and an exempt filing still needs processing.
One status for all three would hide which step holds a filing back.

== Who filed, for whom and under what authority

[[REG-025]]
*REG-025.* Each filing MUST record the actor who submitted it, by an opaque identifier under IAM-091.
It MUST record the party that the actor acted for, a `Party` under DMO-040, and the authority for the filing.
The authority is one of these kinds.

* `own-behalf`: the actor acted for their own party.
* `representation`: a relationship of representation under IAM-061, which the filing names by its id.
* `statutory-power`: a power that a provision gives the actor, which the filing cites.
* `order`: an order of a court or of another authority, which the filing cites with its file number.

At submission, the register MUST check under IAM-050 and IAM-064 that the authority covers the filing type.
The identity specification owns representation, and this rule only records which one applied.

A registrar who acts on a court order and an agent who acts for an owner leave different traces.
A later dispute needs to know which one acted, and on what basis.

== Decisions

[[REG-026]]
*REG-026.* Eligibility, fees, fee exemptions and penalties MUST be decisions under the decisions specification.
A filing MUST name the decision records that it rests on, under DEC-040.
A register MUST NOT compute a fee, an exemption or an eligibility result outside a decision record.

Under section 113 of the amended regulations, the license fee of $24 follows the consumer price index each April 1.
Under section 112, an owner who exercises a right of section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982, pays no fee.
Both rules belong in a decision type with dated parameters, under DEC-037, not in the register.

== Corrections

[[REG-027]]
*REG-027.* A correction MUST name the filing that it corrects, which MUST have a lower sequence.
It MUST carry the full corrected changes and the corrected effective instant.
The corrected filing MUST stay retrievable, under DMO-051.
Once the correction completes, the replay MUST use it in place of the corrected filing for each later record time.

A correction that carries only the difference would force each reader to rebuild the original first.
A whole replacement keeps the replay simple and gives both versions to anyone who asks how the register looked before.

== Orders

[[REG-028]]
*REG-028.* A court order or a registrar order MUST be a filing, with the authority kind `order` or `statutory-power`.
It MUST cite the order or the provision.
It MAY take effect before its submission when its catalog entry allows it.
A register MUST NOT carry out an order by any other means.

The personal property register of British Columbia gives court orders their own change class.
An order that bypasses the filing log leaves the register without a record of its own most sensitive changes.

== Stated changes

[[REG-029]]
*REG-029.* When a filing completes, the register MUST state in it each row that it opens and ends.
An opened row MUST hold its full values, and an ended row MUST name its kind and its entity id.
The catalog entry of the filing type MUST list the kinds of rows that the type may open and end.
The replay MUST apply the stated changes, and MUST NOT run the rules of the register again.

A rule can change after a filing completes, and a replay that runs it again would rewrite history.
The stated changes freeze what the register decided at completion.

== The light chain

[[REG-030]]
*REG-030.* When a filing reaches a final processing status, the register MUST give it the next sequence number.
Sequences MUST start at 1 and leave no gap.
From sequence 2 on, each filing MUST hold in `previousCommitment` the commitment that REG-031 gives the filing before it.
The register MUST complete its filings one at a time, so that each one knows the commitment before it.

A gap or a swap in the sequence then shows at once, inside the register.
The chain needs no key of its own, and no checkpoint or witness of its own.

[[REG-031]]
*REG-031.* The register MUST seal each filing that reaches a final status, and log it under the signed records specification.
The commitment that a filing gets MUST be its entry in the transparency log, the salted hash of LOG-031.
Each register MUST claim at least the `seal-log` profile of LOG-002, and `seal-log-preserve` when it keeps its filings forever.
A register MUST NOT add a signature, a checkpoint or a witness of its own to the chain.

The signed records specification already gives seals, trusted time stamps under LOG-020, a witnessed log and proofs.
A second mechanism in the register would need its own keys, its own monitoring and its own verifier.
The light chain catches gaps cheaply, and the log proves the rest.
A register may make a right opposable from its registration.
The time stamp of the seal is then evidence of that instant.

[[REG-032]]
*REG-032.* A verifier of a register MUST check each of these points, and report each result on its own.

* The sequences run from 1 with no gap.
* Each `previousCommitment` equals the commitment that the filing before it got.
* Each filing that still has its content gives back its commitment, from its salt and its sealed bytes.
* Each destroyed filing has a tombstone that keeps its commitment, under REG-094.
* Each commitment is in the transparency log, under LOG-054.

A verifier that stops at the first failure hides the others.
LOG-054 asks for the same separate reporting.

== Deadlines

[[REG-033]]
*REG-033.* A profile MAY set deadlines for filing types, each counted from the effective date and dated under REG-014.
When a deadline applies, the register MUST record in `deadlineStatus` whether the filing met it.
A register MUST NOT reject a filing only because it came late.
The exception is a profile that cites a provision that bars a late filing.

Section 103 lets a craft run without a transferred license until the 30th day after a transfer.
The impact analysis statement reads it as a 30-day window to apply, yet nothing in it bars a late application.
A register that refused late transfers would leave the wrong owner on record.
