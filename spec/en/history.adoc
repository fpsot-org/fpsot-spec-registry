= Derived state and history
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

The state of a register is what its filings say, taken in order.
Who owns a craft today, and which license it holds, follow from the filings that the register completed.
A register that stores that state apart from its filings can drift from them.
Nobody then knows which one is right.

Two clocks matter.
The valid time tells when a fact holds in the world, such as the day of a sale.
The record time tells when the register knew it.
A retroactive correction changes the past as the register knows it today.
The register must still show what it knew before.
The decisions specification keeps the same two clocks for rule versions, under DEC-035.

== Replay

[[REG-040]]
*REG-040.* Each view of register state MUST equal a replay of the completed filings of the register.
The replay MUST apply the stated changes of REG-029 in order of effective instant, then of sequence.
For each record time at or after a correction, it MUST apply the correction in place of the corrected filing.
A filing that has no `completed` status MUST NOT change the replay.

The fixture `examples/pleasure-craft/snapshots.json` holds six snapshots, and `examples/check-examples.py` rebuilds each one from the filings.
A register that passes this test can rebuild itself after any failure of its stores.

== History rows

[[REG-041]]
*REG-041.* Each row of derived state MUST be one version of an entity, with its history slots.

* `createdByFiling` and `validFrom`: the filing that opened the version and its effective instant.
* `endedByFiling` and `validTo`: the filing that ended the version and its effective instant, once one has.

An ended version MUST stay retrievable, under DMO-051.
The changes inside a filing MUST NOT hold history slots, which only the replay adds.

A row that names its filings answers the question that every registry dispute asks.
The question is which legal act made this fact true, and which one ended it.
The link:https://github.com/bcgov/ppr[personal property register] of British Columbia ties each change to the registration that made it.

== Bitemporal queries

[[REG-042]]
*REG-042.* A register MUST answer each query as of a valid instant V, as recorded at a record instant T.
The answer MUST count only the filings completed at or before T.
It MUST hold the rows whose valid time covers V, and the highest sequence among the filings that count.
The answer MUST follow the class `StateSnapshot` of `model/registry.yaml`.

A court may ask who held a license on the day of an accident.
It may ask for that answer as the register showed it on the day of a search.
The fixture asks about 20 June 2026, as recorded on 30 June.
The answer shows the craft as a destroyed row.
The same day recorded on 10 August shows the corrected hull serial number.

== Stored views

[[REG-043]]
*REG-043.* A register MAY keep stored views of its state, such as tables or search indexes, to answer queries fast.
A stored view MUST be a projection that the register can rebuild from the filings at any time.
When a stored view differs from the replay, the replay wins.
The register MUST then treat the difference as an incident.

A projection is a cache, not a second source of truth.
The incident record tells the operator that some write bypassed the filings, against REG-020.

== No deletion outside a filing

[[REG-044]]
*REG-044.* A register MUST NOT delete a filing, a row or a version except through a destruction filing, under REG-093.
It MUST NOT delete a record only because its period has ended, under DMO-052.

Since version 3.0-alpha, the link:https://registries.govstack.global/5-cross-cutting-requirements.md[GovStack Digital Registries] specification requires the same.
A register keeps the logical record on deletion unless a law requires hard deletion.
The destruction filing is the form that a lawful hard deletion takes here.

== Time-driven and act-driven states

[[REG-045]]
*REG-045.* The lifecycle of each entitlement class MUST be data of its domain pack, with its states and transitions.
Each transition MUST name the filing type or the scheduled event that causes it.
A time-driven state, such as `expired` or `lapsed`, MUST follow from the dates at query time.
It MUST NOT come from a filing.
An act-driven state, such as `suspended`, `revoked`, `cancelled` or `surrendered`, MUST come only from a filing.

Registers of legal entity identifiers tell a lapsed record from a retired one.
A lapsed record is one that nobody renewed in time.
In the fixture, the renewed license reads `expired` on 1 June 2036 with no filing after its renewal.
A register that wrote expiries as filings would need a batch job whose failure would change the law.

== Migration and scheduled expiries

[[REG-046]]
*REG-046.* A register that takes over records from a legacy system MUST bring each one in through a migration filing.
The migration filing MUST name the legacy system and the record that it comes from.
An end date that a law sets for a whole group of entitlements MUST be profile data, under REG-014.
A migration filing MUST open each such entitlement with the end date that the profile gives.

Section 106 of the amended Small Vessel Regulations ends licenses issued before 29 April 2010 on fixed dates.
The dates run in six steps, from 31 March 2026 to 31 December 2030.
The `ca-federal` profile holds that table as `scheduledExpiries`.
A migration filing brings each legacy license in with its end date, and the license then expires with no filing.
