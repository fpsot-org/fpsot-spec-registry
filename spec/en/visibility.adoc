= Visibility, opposability and access
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

Registers differ most on who may see what.
Québec publishes the name of the ultimate beneficiaries of a business, never their date of birth.
It shows their home address only when they declare no professional address.
France lets only people with a legitimate interest read its beneficial owner register.
The pleasure craft license database of Canada is closed, and only enforcement and rescue bodies use it.
A single flag of public or private cannot hold any of these regimes.

A second property stands apart from the first.
Québec makes the name and the domicile of an ultimate beneficiary opposable to third parties, but not the other names.
Visibility tells who may read a field.
Opposability tells whether a party can rely on the field against a third party.
One does not follow from the other.

The link:https://eucrim.eu/news/cjeu-no-unrestricted-access-to-data-of-beneficial-owners/[Court of Justice of the European Union] ruled on 22 November 2022 on beneficial owner data.
It held that open access for the whole public went too far.
Canada and Québec, which that court does not bind, opened public registers in 2023 and 2024.
An engine that hard-coded either answer would be wrong for half its adopters.

== Visibility classes

[[REG-070]]
*REG-070.* Each field of a register MUST have one visibility class, from the widest to the narrowest.

* `public`: anyone.
* `parties`: the parties of the record, their representatives and the agency.
* `agency`: the body that keeps the register and the recipients of an access policy.
* `restricted`: only the roles that the profile names for one purpose.

The domain pack MUST give the default class of each field that it defines.
The profile MAY change it, under REG-015.
A view MUST NOT show a field whose class is narrower than the view.

In the fixture, a public view of the pleasure craft license shows no field at all.
An extract for an enforcement officer shows the license, its validity and its holder.

[[REG-071]]
*REG-071.* The class of a field MAY depend on a condition on another field of the same record.
Such a policy MUST give the class while the condition holds and the class while it does not.

The `ca-qc` profile makes the home address of an ultimate beneficiary public only when no professional address exists.

== Opposability

[[REG-072]]
*REG-072.* Each field MUST also carry an opposability flag, which the profile sets.
A system MUST NOT derive the flag from the visibility class, nor the class from the flag.
A field that neither the pack nor a profile lists is not opposable, under REG-016.

The `ca-qc` profile marks the full name of an ultimate beneficiary public and opposable.
It marks the other names public and not opposable.
Two flags in one record make that difference visible to a reader and to a test.

== Omissions

[[REG-073]]
*REG-073.* A view MUST give a reason code for each field that it leaves out.
The code MUST be one of these.

* `not-public-by-law`: the law or the profile keeps the field from this view.
* `withheld-on-application`: the register withholds the field because a person applied, and the law allows it.
* `unknown-to-register`: the register does not hold the value.
* `destroyed`: a lawful destruction removed the value, under REG-093.

The link:https://standard.openownership.org/en/0.4.0/standard/reference.html[Beneficial Ownership Data Standard 0.4] keeps an exempt subject apart from a fact that the publisher does not know.
A reader who sees an empty field must know whether the law hides it or nobody knows it.

== Access policies

[[REG-074]]
*REG-074.* A view wider than `public` MUST rest on a role in the record or on an access policy.
Each access policy MUST give its legal basis, its recipient category, its purpose and the widest class that it opens.
It MUST also give its expiry when the law limits it, and its duty to log each read.
When the law grants access one request at a time, each grant SHOULD be an entitlement in its own class.

The `ca-federal` profile has three policies, for enforcement officers, search and rescue agencies and the Coast Guard.
France grants access to its beneficial owner register through a certificate of legitimate interest.
Secondary sources report a decree of 2026 that makes it valid three years, which is _to be verified_.
Such a certificate is itself an entitlement, with the requester as its holder.

[[REG-075]]
*REG-075.* A register MUST ask the authorization engine on every request for a view, under IAM-050.
It MUST deny by default, under IAM-051.
The access policies of the profile MUST feed the policies of the engine.
They MUST NOT live only in the code of the register.

== Reads

[[REG-076]]
*REG-076.* Each read of personal information in a register MUST be an audit event under OBS-042.
The event MUST carry the code of the access policy as its purpose, under OBS-095.
That holds for a search, a view, an extract and an export.

The link:https://saaq.gouv.qc.ca/blob/saaq/documents/publications/acces-information/registre-communications-saaq.pdf[register of disclosures of the SAAQ] lists the disclosures of personal information that the body makes.
An audit event for each read gives a register the facts that such a list needs.

[[REG-077]]
*REG-077.* A register SHOULD let a data subject obtain the list of reads of their personal information.
The list SHOULD give the date, the recipient category and the purpose of each read.

The link:https://registries.govstack.global/6-functional-requirements.md[GovStack Digital Registries] specification requires that a data owner can see who read their data.
Estonia makes its registers show citizens who accessed their data, through its link:https://ria.ee/en/state-information-system/people-centred-data-exchange/data-tracker[Data Tracker].
Whether a body may hold back some recipients, such as the police, is a question for counsel.
