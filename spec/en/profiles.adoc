= Jurisdiction profiles
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

The registers that the research compared share their mechanics and split on every legal property.
Québec and Canada publish the beneficial owners of businesses, while France limits that register to people with a legitimate interest.
The law treats a right in the Québec register of movable rights as known to everyone.
A Canadian aircraft registration, by contrast, does not prove title.
The pleasure craft license database is closed to the public, while the aircraft register is public.
No single rule could serve all of them.

This page therefore keeps each legal property in a jurisdiction profile, apart from the core rules.

A profile states the practice of {abbr-fpsot-first}, and it is not legal advice.
Each profile file names the texts that it follows and marks the ones that are _to be verified_.

== Register declarations

[[REG-010]]
*REG-010.* Each register MUST have a declaration in a profile, in the class `RegisterDeclaration` of `model/registry.yaml`.
The declaration MUST give the legal instruments that establish the register, its legal effect and its publicity regime.
It MUST also give its domain pack, the seal profile that it claims and the time zone of its dates.
Each filing names its register, and a register without a declaration takes no filing.

A declaration is the place where a buyer, an auditor and a court read what the register is.
The profile `ca-federal` declares the register of pleasure craft licenses.
The profile `ca-qc` declares two registers whose packs do not exist yet.

== Legal effect

[[REG-011]]
*REG-011.* The legal effect of a register MUST be one of these three values.
The declaration MUST cite its source.

* `administrative-identification`: registration identifies an asset and a party for the administration, and says nothing about title.
* `notice-opposability`: registration makes a right known to third parties, or opposable to them.
* `title`: registration creates or proves title.

A profile of this release MUST NOT declare `title`.
A later release may allow it once counsel names a register in scope whose registration creates or proves title.

Transport Canada states in link:https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/reference-centre/advisory-circulars/advisory-circular-ac-no-202-002[Advisory Circular 202-002] that an aircraft certificate of registration is not a certificate of title.
The link:https://www.rdprm.gouv.qc.ca/fr/documents/publications/10_q_r_rdprm.pdf[questions and answers of the RDPRM] say that the law treats a registered right as known to everyone.
The research found no register in scope whose registration creates title.
Question 1 of xref:questions-for-counsel.adoc[Questions for counsel] asks a lawyer.
The legal effect drives other duties, such as the need for a trusted time stamp at the instant of registration.

== Publicity regime

[[REG-012]]
*REG-012.* The publicity regime of a register MUST be one of `public`, `public-with-exceptions`, `gated` or `closed`, with its source.
The field policies, access policies and search modes of the register MUST agree with it.
A `closed` register MUST NOT make a field public, nor open a search mode to the public.
A public view of a `closed` register MUST show no field.
It MUST give a reason for each field that it leaves out.
It MUST NOT confirm or deny that a record exists, so it gives the same answer to every query.

France alone shows three regimes.
Its national business register shows most fields to the public, with exceptions.
Its beneficial owner register opens only to people with a legitimate interest, and its vehicle register stays closed.
A regime as data lets one engine serve all three.

== Profile files

[[REG-013]]
*REG-013.* Each profile MUST be a file of the class `Profile` of `model/registry.yaml`, in the folder `profiles/`.
Each profile MUST list its sources.
For each source, it MUST state whether the research read it, or mark it `to-be-verified`.
Each parameter of a profile MUST cite one of those sources.
The parameters are the legal instruments, the legal effect, the regime, the terms and the deadlines.
The retention classes, the field policies and the access policies are parameters too.

[cols="2,3,4",options="header"]
|===
|Profile |Applies to |Registers of this release
|`core` |Every register |None. It sets the defaults of REG-016.
|`ca-federal` |Federal registers of Canada |Pleasure craft licenses under the Small Vessel Regulations, as SOR/2025-272 amends them.
|`ca-qc` |Québec registers |The enterprise register and the road vehicle register, whose packs are planned.
|===

A French profile does not exist in this release.
The research read no French code at its source.
The comparison data in `jurisdictions/fr.yaml` therefore describes France without a normative profile.

The `ca-federal` profile follows link:https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-12-31/html/sor-dors272-eng.html[SOR/2025-272], which the research read in full.
A license runs five years from its issue, transfer or renewal, under section 105.
A craft may run without a transferred license until the 30th day after a transfer, under section 103.
Section 106 sets fixed end dates for licenses from before 29 April 2010.
Those dates run from 31 March 2026 to 31 December 2030.
The impact analysis statement says that license information is private and that Transport Canada may not publish it.

== Releases and dated parameters

[[REG-014]]
*REG-014.* Each profile MUST have its own version number under Semantic Versioning 2.0.0, apart from the version of this specification.
Each term and each deadline MUST carry the effective dates to which its value applies.
A filing MUST use the value in force on the date of its effective instant.
That date is in the time zone of its register.
A release that removes or relaxes a parameter of the same profile MUST take a new major version.

Registry law changed in all three jurisdictions between 2022 and 2026.
When France changes the criteria of its beneficial owner register, the result is a profile release, not a breaking change.
The decisions specification dates its parameters in the same way, under DEC-037.

== No law in the core

[[REG-015]]
*REG-015.* A core engine MUST take each legal parameter from the profile of the register.
It MUST NOT hard-code a term, a deadline, a visibility class or an opposability flag.
It MUST NOT hard-code a retention period or a search mode either.
A domain pack MUST NOT hold a value that a law sets.
A profile MUST NOT relax a MUST of the core rules.

A pack holds the mechanics of a domain, such as its filing types and its identifier schemes.
A profile holds the law of a jurisdiction, such as the five-year term of a pleasure craft license.
The same pack then serves every jurisdiction that licenses pleasure craft, each with its own profile.

== Defaults and missing parameters

[[REG-016]]
*REG-016.* Each register MUST also follow the `core` profile, which sets the defaults.
A field that neither the pack nor a profile lists is `restricted` and not opposable.
The seal profile is at least `seal-log`.
A rule may need a parameter that neither the profile nor the defaults give.
The engine MUST then refuse to complete the filing.
It MUST NOT assume a value.

A silent default would turn a gap in a profile into a legal choice that nobody made.
